# Self-play loop v0 — MEW and MACHAMP (2026-09-25)

**Verdict.** The loop runs end to end on the frozen release `eaa5becc54eb`, and two generations were
trained and gated overnight. **Neither was accepted.** Both new PORYGON2 nets passed the human log-loss
gate. Both candidates failed "beats the previous generation" (0.525 and 0.520, both CIs straddle 0.5).
Both passed "does not lose to the human clone" (0.56 and 0.49). The champion is still gen0 (MAG v1 +
DODUO v1 + PORYGON2 v0 inside MILTANK). Self-play ran at **1,721 and 1,943 games per hour** on 4 workers.

The main weakness is the search budget, not the loop. At 500 ms per decision on a shared machine, MILTANK
filled only 33 to 46 playouts per 25-cell matrix. 30 to 41% of cells were empty when the clock ran out, and
36 to 54% of the recorded decisions were too empty to be training targets.

Everything below was played on release `eaa5becc54eb` (the Reg M-C gate release), so none of it is
PRE-GATE.

What the clauses assert:
- **RECORD.** The mixes sum to 1. The recorded value equals x·A·y (worst 4.6e-5). At least 90% of rows carry a

- **PARITY.** Node against Python on the exported files: PORYGON2 worst 1.1e-15, DODUO worst 3.6e-14, for gen1

## 7. Deviations and honest limits

- **The wide-run rule.** The SOLVER brief says a wide run is prepared and handed to Will. This overnight run was
  launched on the coordinator's explicit instruction ("run one to two generations overnight", 4 workers at
  most), through `tools\lownode.cmd`, and never touched the ladder. The command to repeat it is in §8.
- **Not bit-reproducible.** The search is clock-budgeted, so the playout count per decision, and with it every
  sampled move, depends on machine load. Seeds fix the team pairs, the dice and the coins, not the clock. The
  shards are digested in each manifest, so the data used is pinned even though the run cannot be replayed
  bit for bit.
- **The search was starved** (§3), so the self-play mixes are noisy targets. That is the first lever: a longer
  budget or a smaller k per decision, or a pass cap instead of a clock for self-play, which would also make it
  replayable.
- **v0 data only.** Each candidate trained on one generation's 1,600 games (the pre-registered rule). The
  rejected round's games were not pooled with the champion's earlier games.
- **Fixed-n gates, not SPRT.** They were read once at 200 games, as specified. They cannot resolve the effect
  sizes seen here.

**What the numbers point at: the DODUO update, not the value net.**
- With full matrices, 13,752 of 13,907 decisions became DODUO targets (round 1 kept 9,902 of 21,930).
- DODUO moved much further from the human clone: held-out human joint log-loss against v1 rose by +0.046
  [0.041, 0.050] (gen3) and +0.051 [0.046, 0.056] (gen4). Round 1's rise was +0.02.
- **Suspected mechanism** (not yet isolated): the search mix is a distribution over only the 4 rows DODUO itself
  proposed. Training DODUO toward it concentrates DODUO's own head, so the next candidate set is narrower —
  reserved switch rows aside. That is the known failure mode of distilling a pruned search back into its pruner.
- PORYGON2 barely moved. The self-play val MSE against the search value went 0.0239 → 0.0241 (gen3) and
  0.0173 → 0.0163 (gen4), against count-HP at 0.044 and 0.038.
- **Owed:** an ablation that plays each candidate with only one of its two nets updated (new PORYGON2 with
  DODUO v1, and the reverse), which says which net lost the 7 points. After that, a DODUO target that covers
  every legal joint, or a much stronger anchor.

The team-pool, search-budget and depth changes are all in the round-2 pre-registration.
`test-machamp` is GREEN 91/91, and all six deliberate breaks still turn it RED. Parity holds for gen3 and gen4:
PORYGON2 worst 8.9e-16, DODUO worst 2.1e-14.
