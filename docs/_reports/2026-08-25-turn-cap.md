# The turn cap — measured, and it stays at 12. 2026-08-25 (ENGINE).

**Nothing in `engine/medicham2-browser.js` was touched.** The engine did not move, so **no artifact is
withheld** — the three roster stages and `all_mechanics_fire` are untouched and still valid. Census
**701 probed / 701 live / 0 missing** before and after. Damage differential **0 of 6000** at
`--n 6000 --seed 20260804`, all sixteen corners, before and after.

---

## 1. What the cap was, and why

`engine/game_differential.js:56` — `const MAXTURNS = +flag('--turns', 12);`. **It is a command-line
flag with a default of 12, and it always has been** (commit `f60b01c7`, the first whole-game
differential run). There is no cost argument written down beside it and no design note defending 12.
It is the scope choice Will made at the start of this instrument: *"I ONLY CARE ABOUT TURN 1 TO
START"* (`docs/ENGINE.md:17961`).

Deeper caps have been run **twice before** and both times as a side experiment, never as the standing
number: 19 / 30 / 40 on 2026-08-12 (release `ef342837b791`) and 12 vs 30 on 2026-08-19 (release
`94a84744346d`). So "turns 13+ have never been compared" is not quite right — they had been compared,
on a release six days and dozens of fixes old, and the results were never carried into the standing
figure. **Everything quoted since describes the first twelve turns.**

**The register note that real games end around turn 6 holds** — mean 6.8 / 7.0, median 7. Forfeits are
36% of the bo3 store and truncate games early, so they are stripped in the second row of each pair;
the median does not move and the ≤12 share drops by about two points.

A 12-turn cap therefore covers **93–97% of real games**, and the whole tail from 21 to 53 turns is
**66 games out of 17,381 — 0.38%.**

## 3. The number that actually decides it: when a mechanic is FIRST seen to act

Will's rule: *"but like 50 turn games where sides spam protect and encores does not help us."* A long
stall is the same turn repeated; the cap should be set by the turn at which the **last new** thing
happens, not by how long games run.

Nothing recorded that, so it was built. `engine/game_differential.js` now keeps `COV_FIRST_TURN` —
for every census row, the **earliest turn any game was ever observed to credit it** — and publishes
`credit_turn_profile` in the artifact plus a console block. It is purely observational: it reads the
credit events the coverage steering already computes, changes no die, no click and no board, and it
rides in `driverSnap`/`driverRestore` with the other credit maps so a control-arm replay cannot leak
into the primary arm's profile. `credits_with_no_turn_index` must read 0 and does.

**Proof it changed nothing.** The standing configuration re-run with the instrumentation in place is
identical to `git show HEAD:data/game-differential.json` on every headline: mode string, 961 games, 28
diverged, the five class counts, 953/8/0/0/0 end-state verdicts, 27 causes, 17 narration, 10
board-material, 252 rows credited.

**Turns 9 and 12 introduce nothing at all. The curve is flat from turn 6.**

**And the same run at cap 30 confirms it is not an artifact of stopping at 12.** 254 rows credited,
of which exactly **two** are first credited past turn 12 — `ability:poisonsOnMyContact` (turn 13) and
`move:setsOwnTypeAlways` (turn 23). Neither is a late mechanic: both are credited **inside the first
twelve turns** by the other pinned arms of the cap-12 run, whose union credits the same **254** rows
with a **byte-identical** did-nothing list (8 rows) and not-exercised list (17 rows). They landed late
at cap 30 because the coverage-seeking driver got to them late, not because the game has to be long.

**Raising the cap from 12 to 30 buys zero new mechanic coverage.**

**What it does buy is repetition, and the size of it is measurable.** Credit events per turn, cap 30:
26,542 on turn 1, and still 24,754 on turn 13, 22,320 on turn 17, 13,760 on turn 30 — roughly 25,000
per turn all the way down. Turns 13–30 generate **about 340,000 credit events and one new row.**

## 4. So: the cap stays at 12

By Will's own stated criterion the answer is to stop. The curve is flat by turn 6, dead by turn 11,
and a deep run proves nothing is hiding past it. The cost of going deeper is real: the same 961 games
took **95.3 s at cap 12, 195.9 s at cap 16 and 238.8 s at cap 30** — roughly **2.5x** — on every gate
run, for one row that was already covered.

**The default is unchanged. `--turns` remains a flag, so a deep run is always one word away.**

## 5. But depth is NOT free of findings, and this part matters

Coverage of census rows is not the same as coverage of defects, and the two answers point opposite
ways. Same release, same pins, same pool, same 961 games, only the cap differing:

| | cap 12 | cap 16 | cap 30 |
|---|---|---|---|
| protocol PARTED | **28** | 40 | **80** |
| board-material | **10 causes / 10 games** | 17 / 19 | **41 causes / 43 games** |
| narration-only | 17 / 18 | 19 / 20 | 34 / 36 |
| DIFFERENT-END-STATE | 8 | 18 | 31 |
| rows credited (this arm) | 252 | 253 | 254 |
| deepest FIRST credit | turn 11 | turn 13 | turn 23 |
| elapsed | 95.3 s | 195.9 s | 238.8 s |

**The direction was predicted before the run and it rose — that is the instrument reaching further,
not a regression.** The scale was not predicted: **board-material goes from 10 causes to 41.**

**RE-BASELINE, STATED EXPLICITLY.** No figure on one row of that table may be compared with a figure
on another. Three separate reasons, not one: the games run to different lengths, the coverage credit
that STEERS the driver therefore accumulates differently and later clicks differ, and `first_divergences`
is indexed by protocol LINE, which is not comparable across caps at all. The standing published number
— **28 parted, 10 board-material, 17/18 narration, 8 different-end-state** — is a cap-12 number and
stays the standing one.

**What this says about the quarantine gate:** the gate's board-material clause is a claim about the
first twelve turns of a game and nothing else. It is not wrong, and it is narrower than it reads.

**Three families are genuinely turn-13-only** and are the honest answer to "what has never been
looked at": **PP running out**, **a multi-turn counter reaching its end**, and **a forme reverting
when the field effect that forced it expires**. Everything else in the list is a mechanism the cap-12
run already sees; a longer game simply gives it more chances to fire.

**The damage-value rows are the ones that would rank first if this were a fix list** — three separate
hits where the two engines price the same attack differently. They are not late-only. They are what a
2.5x-longer sample surfaces from the same distribution.

**Nothing here was fixed.** Per the brief this is a work list, not a pass.

## 7. The cheaper way to reach the three late-only families

The lab already stages a mechanic alone (`tests/roster.js`, the census probes,
`engine/all_mechanics_fire.js`) and does it in one staged board rather than by paying 18 extra turns on
961 games. A probe that hands a body a 5-PP move and empties it, or one that lets an Infestation count
down, costs milliseconds. **That is where PP exhaustion, counter expiry and forme revert belong** —
not in the cap.
