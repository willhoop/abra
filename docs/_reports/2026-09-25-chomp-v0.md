# CHOMP v0 — the team-preview solver (Reg M-C, open sheets)

2026-09-25 · SOLVER · branch `worktree-agent-a9256d31a5fab9468` · engine release **`eaa5becc54eb`** (post-gate) ·
historical findings record, not a living document.

## 0. Verdict

**CHOMP v0 is built, wired and tested, and it does not pick better brings than a coin.** Scored by PORYGON2 v0 on
the turn-1 position and solved by SLOWKING, its preview **lost to the humans' own recorded bring, 0.430
(0.363–0.499), and the pre-registered SPRT accepted H0** (LLR −3.81 ≤ −2.94). Against the human-modal prior it scored
0.485 (0.417–0.554) and against a random preview 0.510 (0.441–0.578). Both are inconclusive at n = 200. It agrees with
human lead pairs **less often than uniform does** (4.0% against 6.7%). The machinery is sound: the mix is an exact
equilibrium of its own table, and every clause of `solver/tests/test-chomp.js` is green and was shown red on a
deliberate break. The failure is the cell scorer. **Do not ship the CHOMP preview (`rotom.js --preview chomp`)
until a cell scorer beats the human bring.**

## 1. What was built (`solver/chomp/`)

| file | role |
|---|---|
| `options.js` | the 90 options (C(6,4)·C(4,2)); lead order and back order are not separate options |
| `score.js` | all 8,100 cells. MEDICHAM builds the 225 lead-pair battles, where switch-in abilities fire. The public position is read through `prior_adapter.publicState`, and each side's back two are written in as seen bench members (a point belief). PORYGON2 v0 scores the cell through its own `encode` and forward pass. `dmgRange`/`hitProb` are memoised on exactly what they read (≈99.7% hits) |
| `refine.js` | optional MILTANK refinement: CRN playouts (random joints for `depth` turns, PORYGON2 leaf) on the support and best-response cells. Repeats in double-oracle rounds while the support moves onto unplayed cells |
| `chomp.js` | **the standalone interface.** `create({API} or {release})`, then `solve({mine, theirs}, {refine, deadline})` returns the options, `mix`, `oppMix`, `value`, per-option win chances (vs their mix, vs uniform, worst case), the support with labels, the exploitability block and the counters. `sample(r, u)` draws one option |
| `human_prior.js` | the human-modal baseline. A conditional logit on per-species bring and lead rates, plus an exact-team memory. It is fitted on TRAIN players only |
| `data.js`, `plan.js`, `tables.js`, `arms.js`, `report.js` | the header reader and player split, the pre-registration, the sharded table solver, the arena's preview arms and the read-out |

**Wiring.** `solver/rotom/rotom.js --preview chomp` makes CHOMP the first preview choice. The pick is sampled from the
mix and bounded by the preview budget. If CHOMP throws or runs over budget, the choice falls back through the usual
chain and the fallback is counted (`chomp:threw`). ROTOM warms one CHOMP table at startup. The sheet → request-position
mapping moved into `request.js posOfSheet`, so one implementation serves both the old path and CHOMP. The arena gained
`--preview-x/--preview-y` (`human|hprior|random|chomp`), `--plan` (exactly the plan's pairs, with the plan's SPRT read
once at the end) and `--blind` (no interim score).

**The bench assumption was checked, not assumed.** The table builds 225 lead battles, not 8,100. On sampled cells,
`test-chomp` BACK holds every cell equal to PORYGON2 on the full battle of that cell, to within 1e-12.

| policy | v* − its guaranteed value: mean · p50 · p90 |
|---|---|
| **the CHOMP mix** | < 1e-5 (the LP residual; SLOWKING gap ≤ 1e-15) |
| greedy pick vs a uniform opponent | 0.030 · 0.025 · 0.065 |
| best pure option (maximin) — the mixing gain | 0.016 · 0.013 · 0.032 |
| uniform mix | 0.094 · 0.091 · 0.127 |
| the human's actual option | 0.115 · 0.112 · 0.185 |

Support is 2.8 options on average (p90 4). v* is 0.507 on average (p10 0.420, p90 0.598).

**The optional MILTANK refinement** ran on 24 jobs: 12 pairs, both sides, 6×6 plus best responses, 12 playouts per cell, depth 2, PORYGON2 leaf,
k0 = 8, 3 rounds. It played about 89 cells and 1,068 playouts per job and took 26 s on average. It moved the cells by 0.044 on
average (max 0.13) and moved the mix by total variation **0.63**. The unrefined mix is exploitable by **0.029** inside the refined
table. Under a bootstrap of the playouts the final mix's exploitability is 0.023 (p50) and 0.052 (p95). 4.0 support
cells per job were still unplayed after 3 rounds. **The playouts disagree with PORYGON2's cells by more than the whole spread
that separates the options** (greedy 0.030, maximin 0.016). So the equilibrium is determined by noise, not by the matchup.

## 4. Why it fails (diagnosis; the evidence is §3, and the mechanism is not proven)

1. **PORYGON2 v0 has little bring signal.** It learned P(win) from human outcomes, and CHOMP-EV already measured the human bring
   decision at a coin (`docs/_reports/2026-09-23-solver-research-teams-and-meta.md` §1.3). The spread across the 8,100 cells
   is mostly sheet-versus-sheet, which no preview choice can move.
2. **The revealed back-two is off-distribution.** A turn-1 position with four members seen per side never occurs in
   training. The alternative encoding (mode `public`, all four unseen at weight 1/2) cannot see the bring at all. v0 has no
   in-distribution way to show PORYGON2 a bring.
3. **The refinement is too noisy to repair it.** Random-policy playouts at depth 2 move the cells by 0.044 on average, more than the
   0.016 mixing gain.

## 5. What is owed

- **A cell scorer trained for the question**, the research report's T1: a Q-net on (my 4 + leads, their 4 + leads) trained on
  **self-play outcomes** of the in-battle agent (MEW/MACHAMP, M5). Promote it only if it beats the human bring by SPRT in this
  same harness (`plan.js` + `arena.js --plan`).
- A cheaper preview baseline worth testing now: **the human-modal prior** is the strongest arm measured. It is
  inconclusive against random at n = 200, so a larger n is owed before it is called better.
- **Speed.** A table costs 11 s at p50 and 62 s at p90 on this loaded machine (`report.json` `solve_ms`). ROTOM's preview cap is 20 s
  (`--preview-max-ms`), so under load CHOMP would fall back, counted, on a sizeable share of previews.
- `node engine/status.js --write` was **not** run: it must be run from the main checkout, never a worktree.
