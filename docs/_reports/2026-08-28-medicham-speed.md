# MEDICHAM speed — throughput for rollout budgeting

**2026-08-28, MEASURE.** Asked for: how quickly we can play games on MEDICHAM, for the rollout engine.

**This is a TIMING measurement and nothing else.** It makes no claim about whether MEDICHAM is
correct. The simulator is under quarantine for accuracy and nothing here lifts it; a fast wrong engine
is still wrong. No quarantined artifact is quoted — `data/rollout-cost.json` (R2 leaf cost) was not
read and is not the source of any figure below. Every number here was measured on 2026-08-28.

> **REVISED after review.** The first version of this report gave point estimates, and the published
> artifact disagreed with them. It also carried a process-scaling conclusion that my own re-measurement
> then refuted. Both are corrected below and **the retracted numbers are left visible**, in §6 and §7,
> rather than deleted. `data/medicham-speed.json` is now generated by
> `engine/bench_speed_consolidate.js` from every run file, so the artifact and this report cannot drift
> apart again.

## 1. The three numbers — as RANGES

Single process, one core, warm. Engine release **`5f3f7141227c`**, team pool pinned to
**`data/team-pool-frozen`** (8,778 distinct teams; bo3 + ots only, never `games.ladder.jsonl`).

Ranges are across independent runs. **Plan against the LOW end** — the high end needs a process whose
first timed work is the work being measured, which a live search cannot arrange.

| | range | best-conditioned point | pin |
|---|---|---|---|
| **games/sec** | **148.5 – 190.1 whole games/sec** (5.26 – 6.73 ms/game) | 8 runs, 28% spread | turn cap **60**, reaches a real result in **99.9%** of games, mean 10.75 turns |
| **ms/turn** | **0.49 – 0.63 ms/turn** (1,600 – 2,040 turns/sec) | caps 10–60, 2.3–28% spread | flat across caps 10 → 60; the figure that generalises |
| **playouts per second of thinking time** | **157.5 – 174.8/sec** | **4 runs, 11% spread — the tightest figure here** | the leaf MILTANK calls: `rolloutWinProb` n=200, explore=1.0, uniform, **maxTurns 14** |

Restated in the unit a rollout engine spends:

> **One leaf call at MILTANK's shipped settings costs 1.14 – 1.27 seconds.**
> `miltank.js` `DEFAULTS.budgetMs = 20000` therefore buys **16 – 17 leaf calls per decision on one
> core.**

**The leaf range is the one to trust.** All four leaf measurements ran 4,800 playouts each — well past
the tier-up point that wrecks the shorter arms — and they agree to 11%. Everything else in this
document has a wider range and §7 explains why.

At `engine/truncation_curve.js:128-132`'s cell count (K per slot, two slots per side, two sides → **K⁴
cells**), 16–17 leaf calls funds **K=2 (16 cells)** with nothing spare and does not fund **K=3 (81
cells)**, which would need about 95 seconds.

**The tree has already moved, and the pin is why that does not matter.** ENGINE edited `engine/medicham2-browser.js` while this was being written, so `engine_release.js list` now reports **1 of 26 files have moved since** release `5f3f7141227c`. `engine_release.js verify 5f3f7141227c` still reports **intact**, and every arm loaded the frozen copy, so these figures are a measurement OF THAT RELEASE and are PRE-CHANGE with respect to the live tree. **The census is NOT a frozen source and that silently changed the measurement once.**

it. Its documented fallback is **switchRate 0, horizon 0 — the playout then cannot switch at all**,
which is a different and cheaper playout than the one MILTANK ships. The first smoke run printed

declared gap (`requireClosure` walks static `require()` edges and cannot see `fs.readFileSync`). The
census is now read from the live tree, digested, and passed **explicitly** as `switchRate` — which both
functions already take as an argument. Values used: **`maxTurns` 14, `switchRate` 0.0998**, the shipped
ones.

## 3. Fixed cost vs marginal cost

### Fixed — paid once per process

| phase | warmest observed | coldest observed |
|---|---|---|
| bare `node -e 0` process start | 54 ms | 143 ms |
| open the engine release | 19 ms | 45 ms |
| `data/engine-data.js` | 10 ms | 25 ms |
| `medicham2-browser.js` | 50 ms | 106 ms |
| `board.js` + `rollout_leaf.js` | 8 ms | 18 ms |
| **the Showdown dex** (`Dex.forFormat`) | **386 ms** | **1,280 ms** |
| **total to engine-ready** | **475 ms** | **1,485 ms** |
| team pool (cached) | 114 ms | 802 ms |
| `require('game_differential.js')` | 485 ms | 7,660 ms |

- **The dex is 81% of the fixed cost.** Everything ABRA owns loads in ~90 ms.
- **`game_differential.js` is HARNESS cost, not engine cost.** This benchmark requires it only to reach
  `buildPair`/`freshBodies`, the repo's one sheet→body converter. A rollout engine driving
  `rolloutWinProb` off a live `Board` never pays it. It is excluded from every per-game figure.

**Amortisation:** at ~175 playouts/sec the 475 ms warm fixed cost is worth **≈83 playouts**. A process
that plays fewer than ~100 playouts is more than half startup. (And §7: a process needs ~4,000 playouts
before it is running at full speed at all, which is the far bigger reason to keep workers alive.)

### Memory — the number that constrains parallelism

| after loading | RSS |
|---|---|
| bare node | 39 MB |
| + release + `engine-data.js` + `medicham2` + `board` + `rollout_leaf` | 61 MB |
| **+ the Showdown dex** | **132 MB** |
| + the team pool (harness only) | 197 MB |
| **a warmed worker mid-run** | **385 MB** |

The Showdown dex is **71 MB of the 132 MB engine-ready worker**; everything ABRA owns is about
**22 MB**.

### Marginal — paid per unit of work

| unit | cost |
|---|---|
| one TURN of simulation | **0.49 – 0.63 ms** |
| one PLAYOUT to cap 14 | 5.17 – 5.87 ms simulation |
| one whole GAME to a result (cap 60) | 5.26 – 6.73 ms simulation |
| rebuilding four fresh bodies a side (`freshBodies`) | 0.037 – 0.048 ms per playout — under 1% |
| **leaf seeding** — `buildSide` ×2 + field/side-state, per SAMPLE | **~0.55 ms, ~9% of the leaf** |

The seeding line matters for a shallow search: it is **flat in the horizon**, so it does not shrink
when you shorten the rollout. At cap 6 it would be roughly a quarter of the leaf.

## 4. Turn-cap scaling and the turn distribution

Playout arm, `runPlayout` at explore=1.0 / uniform, 40 team pairs from the pinned pool, 1,000 playouts
per rep. Simulation time only. **Range across runs**, from `data/medicham-speed.json`.

| turn cap | playouts/sec | ms per turn | mean turns | truncated | runs | spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 94.3 – 283.4 | 0.596 – 1.791 | 5.92 | 94.2% | 4 | 200% — **contains an un-warmed arm, read `all`** |
| 10 | 199.6 – 204.2 | 0.557 – 0.570 | 8.79 | 55.5% | 3 | 2.3% |
| 12 | 179.6 – 194.2 | 0.536 – 0.580 | 9.61 | 35.9% | 4 | 8.1% |
| **14 (shipped)** | **170.3 – 193.4** | **0.512 – 0.582** | 10.10 | 20.9% | 5 | 13.6% |
| 20 | 42.7 – 188.7 | 0.500 – 2.208 | 10.61 | 3.8% | 4 | 342% — **contains an un-warmed arm** |
| 60 | 148.5 – 190.1 | 0.489 – 0.627 | 10.75 | 0.1% | 8 | 28% |

**ms/turn sits in 0.49–0.63 for every cap from 10 up.** That is what makes "ms per turn" the honest
planning unit: cost is linear in depth. The two 300%+ rows each contain one arm that never reached
steady state — the artifact's `all` array names the file for every endpoint.

Cap 6 is genuinely worse per turn even when warm (0.596 vs 0.489 at cap 60): **turn 1 is more expensive
than a later turn** — switch-in effects, ability activation — so a very shallow rollout pays a worse
rate per turn, not a better one.

### Turn distribution actually timed (cap 60, n = 1,000 playouts)

```
mean 10.75   min 2   p25 8   p50 10   p75 13   p90 16   p99 23   max 60
```

**These playouts are about 45% LONGER than real games**, which is expected rather than a defect. Read
straight out of `data/rollout-switch-census.json` (`stores[].length_no_forfeit`):

| | n | mean | p50 | p90 | p99 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ladder | 35,083 | 7.11 | 7 | 10 | 16 |
| bo3 | 7,735 | 7.74 | 7 | 11 | 16 |
| **these playouts (cap 60)** | 1,000 | **10.75** | **10** | **16** | **23** |

The census's own note says why: *"a uniformly-random playout is less lethal than a person"*. So any
per-GAME figure here is a claim about a uniform-random 4v4 playout, not about a real game; the ms/turn
figure is unaffected, which is another reason to quote the turn.

## 5. The leaf — the path MILTANK actually pays for

`engine/miltank.js` `DEFAULTS` (miltank.js:484) are `n: 200, explore: 1.0, foePolicy: 'uniform',
turns: <census>`, and what it calls is `rollout_leaf.rolloutWinProb`. Each of the n samples pays
`buildSide` **twice** — fresh bodies every sample, because MEDICHAM mutates what it is handed — then
`runPlayout`.

Measured on 24 turn-0 boards built from pinned-pool teams, 4,800 playouts per arm, **4 independent runs
at cap 14**:

| cap | ms per LEAF CALL | leaf calls/sec | playouts/sec | ms/turn | mean turns | truncated | runs | spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **14 (shipped)** | **1,144 – 1,270** | 0.79 – 0.87 | **157.5 – 174.8** | 0.607 – 0.673 | 9.43 | 13.8% | 4 | **11%** |
| 60 | 1,197 – 1,447 | 0.69 – 0.84 | 138.2 – 167.1 | 0.612 – 0.740 | 9.78 | 0.0% | 2 | 21% |

One observation that is not a speed claim: **the shipped horizon of 14 truncates 13.8% of leaf
playouts.** A truncated playout is scored on HP totals, so roughly one leaf sample in seven is a
material comparison rather than a result. Going to cap 60 removes that entirely and costs about 5%
more time. Whether that is worth it belongs to leaf calibration, which is WITHHELD.

### What a thinking budget buys

Single process, cap 14, n=200, using the range:

| thinking budget | playouts | leaf calls at n=200 |
|---|---|---|
| 1,000 ms | 158 – 175 | 0.8 – 0.9 |
| 5,000 ms | 788 – 874 | 3.9 – 4.4 |
| 15,000 ms | 2,363 – 2,622 | 11.8 – 13.1 |
| **20,000 ms (miltank default)** | **3,150 – 3,496** | **15.8 – 17.5** |

## 6. Process scaling — MY EARLIER CONCLUSION WAS WRONG AND IS RETRACTED

### RETRACTED

> *"Aggregate throughput is flat at roughly 145–220 playouts/sec no matter how many processes you run.
> Eight processes deliver what one and a bit deliver. … Do not plan on multi-process throughput."*

That came from `data/_bench-scaling.json`, which reads:

| workers | 1 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| aggregate playouts/sec | 167.1 | 172.7 | **143.2** | **144.3** | 219.2 |

**Two of those rows are impossible.** Four processes cannot do less total work than one. That is not
sub-linear scaling, it is collapsed legs — and I read it as a finding. The consolidator now detects it
mechanically (`process_scaling.arms[].impossible_rows`), so the same mistake cannot be made from the
artifact again.

I also dismissed the unwarmed BelowNormal arm outright. **It was not the one that was wrong.** Its
absolute rates are depressed by tier-up (1 worker at 108.5/sec against 191.6 warmed) but its *ratio*,
1w → 4w = 2.77x, agrees with the corrected measurement.

### CORRECTED — warmed AND interleaved

Re-measured with worker counts **repeated in interleaved order** (`1,2,4,1,2,4`), so a drift in machine
conditions cannot alias onto worker count. Each child warmed with 3,000 discarded playouts.
`data/_bench-scaling-interleaved.json`:

| workers | leg A | leg B | best | speedup | per-worker | children within a leg |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 191.6 | **26.2** | 191.6 | 1.00x | 191.6 | — |
| 2 | 276.7 | 271.2 | 276.7 | **1.44x** | 138.3 | 138.2 / 138.5 |
| 4 | 449.7 | 288.8 | 449.7 | **2.35x** | 112.4 | 113.7 / 113.0 / 111.8 / 111.2 |

**MEDICHAM does scale across processes, sub-linearly: about 1.44x at 2 workers and 2.35x at 4.**

**Confidence is LOW and the reason is on the table above.** The two 1-worker legs differ by **7.3x**
(191.6 vs 26.2) while the two 2-worker legs agree to 2%. That is the sporadic collapse of §7, and it is
why one leg per condition is worthless — which is precisely how the retracted table was produced.

Children *within* a leg are uniform to ±2%, so whatever limits throughput is shared and smooth; it is
the leg-to-leg variation that is wild.

### Context that survives the retraction

- **The machine does scale.** Two throwaway controls with tiny working sets, same spawn driver, same
  box, minutes later: CPU-bound **1.97 / 3.69 / 5.21 / 6.61x** at 2/4/6/8 workers; allocation-churning
  **1.88 / 3.67 / 4.88 / 5.58x**. MEDICHAM's 2.35x at 4 workers is well short of the control's 3.69x,
  so there is a real gap — just not the total absence I claimed.
- **The workers are not CPU-starved.** During the 6-worker leg each worker received **1.17 cores** over
  a 15-second sample, 9.36 cores busy of 8 physical.
- **RAM binds higher up.** 385 MB per warmed worker; the 12-worker leg took free memory to **1.73 GB**
  and walls to four minutes.
- **Leading hypothesis for the gap:** a 385 MB working set against a **16 MB L3** on a dual-channel
  laptop part. **Hypothesis, not a finding** — see OWED §2.

**Practical guidance: budget ONE worker's worth (~175 playouts/sec) and treat extra workers as upside
rather than as budget.** 2 workers is a defensible 1.4x. Nothing above 4 has a trustworthy point.

## 7. Noise floor, warm-up, priority — and what item 3 turned out to be

The review asked why five bench files span 151.5 → 190.1 at cap 60 when I had claimed a 2.5% noise
floor. **The 2.5% figure was right about the wrong thing, and quoting it as though it described the
between-run spread was my error.**

### The corrected framing

| quantity | value | what it describes |
|---|---|---|
| **within-arm noise floor** | **2.5%** | reps 3–9 of a 10-rep single-arm probe, one process, steady state |
| **between-run spread** | **8 – 28%** for arms that reached steady state | different processes |
| **between-run spread** | **up to 342%** for arms that did not | different processes |

`data/_bench-repeat.json`, cap 60, 10 reps in one process:

```
119.7  161.6  171.7  176.1  174.0  173.5  173.7  174.6  172.0  65.5   playouts/sec
```

Reps 3–9 span 171.7–176.1. **That 2.5% band is reps within one arm and nothing else.**

### Cause 1 — V8 tier-up, a 5.6x effect over ~4,000 playouts

The first timed arm of a fresh process, rep by rep of 1,000 playouts:

```
ms per turn:  2.89 → 1.78 → 1.67 → 1.58 → 0.68 → 0.51
```

Reproduced in two independent runs, always on whichever cap ran **first** and never on a later one. The
harness's warm-up plays only ~36 playouts against the ~4,000 the curve needs, so a short run publishes a
partly-cold number: cap 20 as the first arm of a 3-rep run reads **42.7** playouts/sec against **188.7**
as a later arm elsewhere.

### Cause 2 — sporadic collapse to a third or a seventh

Rep 10 above fell to 65.5. The two 1-worker scaling legs read 191.6 and 26.2. It happens on an
otherwise idle box, has no reproducible trigger, and is the reason every figure in this report is
**fastest-of-N**.

### What it is NOT

- **NOT the cap, and NOT arm position alone.** Running the same six caps in **descending** order
  (`data/_bench-reverse.json`) made **every cap faster** than the ascending run — cap 60 moved from last
  to first and went 151.5 → 190.1, cap 6 moved from first to last and went 240.1 → 283.4. Three further
  runs put cap 60 second behind caps 6, 12 and 20 and read 165.7, 173.1 and 148.5, with no monotone
  relation to the preceding cap.
- **NOT contention.** The fastest and the slowest legs both ran with 6+ GB free and under 10%
  background CPU.
- **NOT priority.** See below — the earlier "0.4%" claim was too tight and is corrected.

### Priority — corrected bound

The **only** like-for-like comparison is same cap set, same order, same instrument:

| priority | cap 14 | cap 60 |
|---|---|---|
| BelowNormal (`tools\lownode.cmd`), `_bench-belownormal.json` | 170.3 | 158.5 |
| NORMAL (direct `node`), `_bench-normal.json` | 170.9 | 151.5 |

**Under 5%, with the sign flipping between caps — so no priority effect is detectable at a bound of
about 5%,** not the 0.4% I first published (that quoted only the cap-14 half). The review's example —
`_bench-normal` 151.5 against `_bench-headline2` 180.6 — compares **different cap sets** and is not a
priority comparison at all.

### Residual, unattributed

**Part of the spread I cannot account for, and I would rather say so than invent a mechanism.** Two runs
with cap 6 as the **first** arm read 94.3 and 240.1 — 2.5x apart at the same arm position, with 3 and 5
reps. Two candidate heuristics (total playouts in the arm; total turns in the arm) were tried against the
data and both were contradicted by it.

### Instrument consequence

**Measure one cap per process, as the first timed arm, with at least 6 reps, and repeat every condition
at least twice in interleaved order.** The multi-cap sweeps published first violated all three.

### Machine

**AMD Ryzen 7 7735HS — 8 physical cores, 16 threads**, nominal 3.2 GHz, single-thread boost 4.75 GHz,
14.3 GB, node v24.15.0, Windows 11. Under multi-worker load the package ran at **86–95% of nominal**.
Brave held ~1.4–2.9 cores throughout. A background agent was editing `engine/quarantine.js`, `docs/` and
`CHANGELOG.md` during the first leg only.

## 8. What this number does NOT cover

1. **It is not an accuracy claim.** MEDICHAM is quarantined for correctness.
2. **Any per-game figure is a claim about ONE turn cap.** Name the cap or do not quote the number.
3. **The playouts are ~45% longer than real games** (mean 10.75 vs a human non-forfeit 7.11/7.74).
4. **The leaf figures are TURN-0 boards** — four live bodies a side, full HP, the most expensive leaf.
   A mid-game leaf is cheaper by an unmeasured amount.
5. **Mechanics coverage is whatever the pinned pool brings.** Usage-weighted, not the census roster;
   `game_differential`'s closet drops every Zoroark/Zorua team, so **no Illusion is in the sample**.
6. **It is the cost of the LEAF, not of a MILTANK decision** — no board construction, feature
   extraction, MAG scoring or candidate enumeration.
7. **One machine, and not an idle one.**
8. **The pool is bo3 + ots only.** `games.ladder.jsonl` is not in `data/team-pool-frozen`.
