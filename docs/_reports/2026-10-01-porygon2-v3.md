## Verdict

- **A. The design is written** (`solver/porygon2/v3/DESIGN.md`). Every citation was re-read at the source. **Six
  premises in the brief were wrong or overstated** and are corrected:
  1. **MuZero.** Its n-step value bootstrapping is for Atari. In board games MuZero bootstraps to the end of the game,
     i.e. the outcome.
  2. **KataGo does not blend** a search value into its main value target. It adds auxiliary short-horizon heads.
  3. **Playout-cap randomisation does not make cheap labels.** Only full-search turns are recorded for training.
  4. **piKL anchors to a human-imitation policy**, so a search-imitating policy head cannot be its prior.
  5. **"λ 0.03 is harmless" is not shown.** Its SPRT point estimate is 0.469.
  6. **Quiescence is built** (`quiesce 'all'`, 1.23.0) **but has never been measured in games.** It "fixes" nothing yet.

  on the search's ROOT value.

## 1. Corrections to the brief (A)

Full table: `DESIGN.md`, *Corrections*. What was checked, and where:
- MuZero: arXiv 1911.08265, PDF appendix *Training*: "For board games, we bootstrap directly to the end of the game,
  equivalent to predicting the final outcome; for Atari we bootstrap for n = 10 steps". Reanalyze uses n = 5.
- KataGo: `docs/KataGoMethods.md`, *Short-term Value and Score Targets*. The auxiliary heads use horizons of about 6, 16
  and 50 turns, and the main head predicts "the final game result". The paper (arXiv 1902.10565) §3.1 gives
  playout-cap randomisation, where "only turns with a full search are recorded for training" (p = 0.25). Table 2 gives
  the ablation factors: ownership and score 1.65×, game-specific features 1.55×.
- piKL: arXiv 2112.07544, abstract. It regularises toward "an imitation-learned policy".
- AlphaZero: arXiv 1712.01815 (z is the value target; π is the policy target). AlphaGo, *Nature* 529, from the authors'
  text: MSE 0.37 test against 0.19 train when trained on whole games, and 0.226 / 0.234 on 30 million positions, one per
  game. ExIt: arXiv 1705.08439 §4.2 (one state per game) and §5.2 (more than 10^5 samples).
- Maia (nine models, 1100–1900) and Maia-2 (skill-aware attention on active and opponent skill, over-sampling unequal
  games, value head): the arXiv PDFs.
- Hinton, Vinyals, Dean: arXiv 1503.02531, abstract. NNUE: Nasu 2018, the English translation (D. Klein,
  `asdfjkl/nnue`). The network was sized so that "the amount of positions which can be evaluated per time unit is equal
  to" Sankoma-Kankei. Shannon 1950 (an evaluation "can only be applied in relatively quiescent positions"): the text
  hosted by the University of Pavia.
- Crossref metadata: Sutton 1988, *Machine Learning* 3:9–44; Tesauro 1995, *CACM* 38(3):58–68; Smith and Winkler 2006,
  *Management Science* 52(3):311–322.

### 2.2 The deep reference

`node solver/porygon2/v3/evalset.js label --release eaa5becc54eb --workers 8 --k 4 --r 8 --cap 30` (`labels.summary.json`).

- **The root is MILTANK's.** It uses gen5's DODUO ranking, 4 × 4 candidates, one reserved switch row, the mega
  reservation and honest worlds.
- **Each cell is played to the end** by gen5's prior argmax on both sides, with common random numbers (`deep.js`). There
  is no value net in it. MILTANK's own leaf is gen5's net, so a "large pass budget" MILTANK would have scored gen5 as
  agreeing with itself, and this departs from the brief's wording on purpose.
- **Counts.**
  - 166,144 playouts and 626,352 turns (3.77 per playout).
  - 30 playouts hit the 30-turn cap (0.018%).
  - 0 errors and 0 forced roots.
  - 223 of 1,247,556 policy calls found no prior mass on any joint (the first joint was played). This is counted.
- **Cost.** About 0.15–0.2 s of CPU per playout and 128 playouts per position, so about 20–25 s of CPU a position. The
  run took 3 h 25 min wall on 8 BelowNormal workers.
- **One bug was caught and fixed before any label was kept.** The first run stepped a side-B cell as (mine, theirs), but
  the engine order is (A, B). It threw "switch to team[k] which cannot come in" on 5 of 9 positions. All its labels were
  discarded. `test-porygon2-v3-evalset.js` ORIENT pins the order, and `P2V3_DEEP_BREAK=orient` turns it RED.
- **The reference is noisy, and the figures say so.** 529 of the 1,300 positions are FLAT: every row has the same deep
  value, so the game is already decided under every row. On the 771 informative positions, the reference's split-half
  agreement (4 playouts against 4) is τ 0.243 and top-1 0.435. The nets are scored against the full 8-playout label, so
  their agreement is not bounded by that split-half figure. It measures the reference's reliability, not a ceiling.

**What the calibration says.** The ladder's +0.19 is not a product of the search's max over rows; the raw net shows it.
Nor is it the net misreading the game in general: the same nets are calibrated on strong human positions. It sits on

for gen5 (`report-*.json`) shows every bin from 0.5 to 0.9 winning 13–24 points below its value. This is a reading of
these figures, not a tested cause, and it is owed (OWED, item 3).

## 3. The student (C)

`python solver/porygon2/v3/student.py teacher | train | export` (`solver/porygon2/v3/model/porygon2-v3-student.metrics.json`).

- **The teacher.** v2's checkpoint (`runs/k1/B/model.pt`, the source of `porygon2-v2-k1.json`). Its value logit and its
  pooled auxiliary distributions were computed once over every TRAIN and VAL row of v2's own tensors. TEST rows were
  never read: the evaluation set's bo1 games are v2 TEST games.
- **The student.**
  - It reads the same inputs as v2, so v2's `features.js` encoder serves both.
  - It has shared member MLPs (v2's embeddings and numbers → 48 → 32), mean and max pooling, side, field and rating
    codes, and a 64-unit head. It is antisymmetric by construction and uses no attention. 39,892 parameters.
  - Loss: binary cross-entropy against sigmoid(teacher logit) (Hinton's soft target), plus 0.25 × the pooled auxiliary
    heads.
  - Data: every position of every TRAIN game, 343,052 rows, with VAL selection on 48,000 rows.
  - 12 epochs; epoch 11 was selected (VAL KL 0.00177, logit MSE 0.043).
- **Wiring.** `solver/porygon2/leaf.js` dispatches arch `v3-student` to `solver/porygon2/v3/leaf.js` (ratings
  [1600, 1600], v2's query). The league spec is `solver/porygon2/v3/gen5-p2v3s.json`. `rollout.js`'s `leafByModel` and
  `leafOwn` count it per file, the 1.50.0 counter. No arm names it.
- **The pre-registered rule** (`solver/porygon2/v3/preregistration.json`, written at 05:30Z before any scoring):

| clause | measured | result |
|---|---|---|
| ranking: Δτ CI lower bound > 0, or Δregret CI upper bound < 0; and Δtop-1 ≥ 0 | Δτ [−0.0067, +0.0168]; Δregret [−0.0055, +0.0007]; Δtop-1 +0.0023 | **FAIL** |
| calibration: ΔECE ≤ 0 with CI upper bound < +0.005, and \|gap\| ≤ gen5's | ΔECE −0.0129 [−0.0247, +0.0060]; \|gap\| 0.083 against 0.102 | **FAIL** (upper bound +0.0060) |
| cost: median ratio ≤ 1.00 | 0.91 | PASS |

**So no 14 s screen and no SPRT are pre-registered.** The student does as well as v2 or better on every figure, at 0.35×
v2's cost. That is the useful result: **distillation kept v2's quality and removed its cost.** But the frozen set cannot
tell any of these nets apart from gen5's on ranking, and it cannot tell the student apart on calibration at the
pre-registered tolerance.

## 5. What the evidence says for v3 (DESIGN §1–§7)

- **Cost is the lever that can be pulled now.** A student at 0.91× gen5's cost scores as well as or better than v2. So
  the next leaf SPRT can test the value question at equal cost, which SPRT 2 could not: it gave up 2.6× the leaves.
- **Ranking agreement at depth 0 does not separate these nets.** The differences are within ±0.008 τ, and the reference
  is noisy (8 playouts per cell; 41% of positions are flat). To be useful for v3, the reference needs more playouts per
  cell on the informative positions, or a stronger continuation policy (DESIGN §6).
- **The ladder overconfidence is in the net, on our positions, and not on strong human positions.** The fix that targets
  it is how our agent converts positions, or the world it rebuilds. It is not human-data calibration.
