MEW

the self-play data engine — ABRA plays the official Champions engine against itself
What MEW is for. ABRA has 1,061 usable human games, which can only detect an edge of about 4.3 points over a coin flip. Resolving a 2-point effect needs roughly 4,900. Human replays arrive at ~330 clean games a day, so most of ABRA's results are stuck inside that blind spot.

MEW removes the limit for questions about the game — it generates unlimited battles on the same engine Showdown runs, verified against the Smogon calculator on 31 damage scenarios. It says nothing about people: not whether players tech for the metagame, not whether ladder rating predicts anything. Those still need real games, and self-play games are kept in a separate file so the two can never mix.
Teams are real six-Pokémon teams observed on the ladder, not invented ones — VGC's "starting position" is a team, and the inhabited part of that space is the metagame. Unrevealed moves are sampled from measured usage priors. These are plausible reconstructions of real teams, not the real sets.

Provenance. Engine —, format —, policy —. Every self-play record carries the engine commit, format, policy and seed, so a game can always be traced to what produced it — and a run reproduces from its seed.

Policy note. These battles use the random policy. That is deliberate for now: it is unbiased and it proves the pipeline. It is not what a trained agent looks like, and it must not be used to train a value net — a model learns "P(win) when both players move at random", which is not a question anyone has. The behaviour-cloned policy is the next step.
